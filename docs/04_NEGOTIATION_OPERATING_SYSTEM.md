# THE NEGOTIATOR — HUMAN NEGOTIATION OPERATING SYSTEM
Version: 1.0

## 1. Purpose
Standardize how human Negotiators represent customers while preserving professional relationships with businesses.

## 2. Negotiator Responsibilities
- Understand customer objective
- Confirm authority
- Research relevant market information
- Review internal partner information
- Establish negotiation strategy
- Communicate professionally
- Protect customer interests
- Preserve business relationships
- Document every material negotiation step
- Present accurate offers

## 3. Negotiation Brief
Each case should establish:
- Customer objective
- Product/service
- Seller/business
- Publicly observed price
- Customer target
- Customer maximum
- Timing
- Quantity
- Must-have conditions
- Flexible conditions
- Acceptable alternatives
- Walk-away conditions
- Authority limits

## 4. Negotiation Concepts
Negotiators should be trained in:
- BATNA
- Reservation point
- Target outcome
- Opening position
- Anchoring
- Concessions
- Trade-offs
- Bundling
- Value creation
- Timing
- Relationship management
- Objective criteria

## 5. Negotiation Sequence
1. Prepare
2. Establish contact
3. Confirm availability
4. Establish customer intent
5. Request best available offer
6. Evaluate
7. Counter where justified
8. Trade concessions
9. Seek additional value
10. Confirm final terms
11. Obtain evidence/confirmation
12. Present to customer

## 6. Price Is Not the Only Variable
Negotiate:
- Price
- Quantity
- Delivery
- Installation
- Warranty
- Upgrades
- Add-ons
- Payment terms
- Cancellation
- Scheduling
- Service levels
- Bundles

## 7. Customer Authority
Never exceed a customer's explicit maximum without approval.

If a business offers:
> $520

and customer maximum is:
> $500

the Negotiator must seek approval before accepting $520.

## 8. Counteroffer Rules
A counteroffer should have a reason.
Avoid arbitrary repeated discount requests.
Use:
- Volume
- Timing
- Immediate payment
- Repeat business
- Package size
- Flexible scheduling
- Competitive alternatives
- Existing relationship where appropriate

## 9. Confidentiality
Internal commercial agreements and negotiation intelligence are confidential.

## 10. Prohibited Conduct
Never:
- Invent competing bids
- Falsify customer information
- Threaten businesses
- Misrepresent authorization
- Make fraudulent claims
- Hide material conditions
- Pressure vulnerable customers
- Promise an outcome before it exists

## 11. Escalation
Escalate when:
- Value exceeds defined threshold
- Legal terms change materially
- Business relationship is at risk
- Customer requirements conflict
- Dispute occurs
- Negotiator lacks authority
- Safety/compliance concern exists

## 12. Quality Standard
A good negotiation is not necessarily the largest discount.
It is the best legitimate outcome consistent with customer objectives, business acceptance and sustainable relationships.
